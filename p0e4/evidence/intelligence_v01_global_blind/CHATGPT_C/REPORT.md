# CHATGPT_C — independent research V01
Observation date: 22 September 2026. Execution: GPT-based Codex using public web search/open, not the consumer ChatGPT browser interface. Research freeze preserves these bytes, not the validity of the claims.

The attainable evidence supports a reference library and controlled creative experiments, not a winning tempo, key, chorus deadline, or hit probability. The sample contains 42 song entities and 49 success observations. Seven songs have partial musical metadata, all secondary; none has a measured vocal range or private audience retention. No calibrated musical-performance association can be estimated.

## Independence
Only the frozen research prompt was read from project files. No sibling track, earlier research, repository architecture, memory file, private analytics, or existing creative asset was consulted. Unavoidable exposure: the agent context already contained general project/governance instructions and an injected memory summary. These were not used to choose songs or infer artist characteristics. General pretrained musical knowledge exists but is not entered as measurement.

## METHODOLOGY
The unit of success observation is a named chart track × source-defined period × market/platform. The song table is a deduplicated title/artist entity registry, not proof of a unique recording master. Exact ISRC, recording hashes and official-upload mappings are unknown. No audio was downloaded.

GLOBAL_SUCCESS uses the complete publicly listed IFPI 2024 top 20 and 2025 top 10. That supplies 30 observations, with repeats across years retained for longevity context but not counted as new songs. HINDI_INDIAN_SUCCESS uses the ten named high-streaming tracks in Indian Express's Spotify India 2023 report, plus Spotify's explicit local 2024 winner. Only stated winners receive rank 1; the order of the other nine is not converted to ranks. This is an India-consumption cohort, not a claim that all songs or artists are Hindi or Indian.

GLOBAL_HINDI_CROSSOVER uses five tracks named in Spotify's 2024 Indian export account and three named Bollywood diaspora successes in its 2023 account. This is a broad Indian-export proxy. It does not establish Hindi-only crossover, non-diaspora adoption, or mass-market penetration. All export ranks and counts remain null. UNCHAINED_NITIN_OWN_DATA contains zero observations.

This is the largest directly transcribed bounded sample in this run, not the largest available universe. Its 2023–2025 concentration prioritizes recent evidence but does not cover the requested full 10–15-year window. Extending chart lists without reliable recording-level features would enlarge the success list without improving musical inference. Historical 2014 structural reporting was inspected for context, not merged into contemporary measurements.

Musical enrichment deliberately selected six contrasting reference candidates plus the detailed public HSD case. This is purposive missingness, not random coverage. SongBPM and Tunebat fields are estimates copied from identifiable song pages. They are not analyst listening judgments. SongBPM cites Spotify upstream; matching values across vendors are not independent validation. Arrangement metadata is separated from recording metadata. Major/minor labels are not raga identifications.

All populated feature cells carry a source URL, source ID, version scope, acquisition date, method and qualitative confidence. Audio timestamps are null unless the secondary source reports a timed boundary. Null means unavailable; it never means zero. Script outputs count missing cells and display denominators. CSV exports flatten JSON without filling gaps.

## GLOBAL_FINDINGS
The global cohort establishes exceptional annual commercial outcomes under IFPI's source definition. It does not identify which music caused them. IFPI aggregates versions and weights formats/regions; its 2024 method change prevents naïve comparison with older annual equivalents. These are not literal unweighted Spotify streams. [IFPI methodology](https://www.ifpi.org/benson-boones-beautiful-things-confirmed-by-ifpi-as-biggest-selling-global-single-of-the-year/)

A useful external structural check is HSD's nine non-holiday US number-one songs in 2025: reported average length 3:29, average tempo 87 BPM, and 33% with first chorus later than a minute. Its detailed luther case reports a 1:09 first chorus. This refutes a universal early-chorus requirement; it does not show delayed choruses improve retention. The US selection is contextual, not a global estimate. [HSD public report, pages 2, 6, 11–13](https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Hit-Songs-Deconstructed-2025-1-Hit-Report.pdf)

Beautiful Things illustrates measurement risk: a database and a published audition arrangement supply incompatible pulse descriptions. The canonical BPM is deliberately null. It would be misleading to average these values or impose one on Nitin's song. [Database](https://songbpm.com/%40benson-boone/beautiful-things-geazf), [arrangement](https://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/benson-boone/beautiful-things/MN0307720_D1)

## VOCAL_KEY_OPTIMIZER
Nitin's range, comfortable tessitura, register transition, sustainable climax and fit score are unknown. Measure them from newly recorded, authorized audition material. Start with an easy conversational phrase, then the original proposed chorus and climax at moderate intensity. Use a keyboard or verified pitch tracker to log actual pitches, duration at each pitch, vowel, register, breath demand and self-rated effort. Avoid treating the highest isolated note as the sustainable range.

Begin in a comfortable chosen key. Transpose the complete arrangement and melody together in small semitone steps within the singer's comfortable zone; retain mode, intervals, contour, tempo, groove and section placement. Record comparable takes, rest between them, and stop a take when uncomfortable. Compare pitch stability, diction, tone, register transitions and repeatable chorus delivery on another occasion. Nitin selects creative character with Vatsal after hearing the takes. Range alone cannot determine fit. Until repeatable observations exist, return null, never a fabricated numerical score.

A transposition preserves every interval when the same integer semitone offset is added to all note pitches. It does not necessarily preserve instrumental timbre, vocal register behavior or production quality; those require audition. A change of mode or selective octave rewrite is a separate arrangement decision.

## UNCERTAINTIES_LIMITATIONS
All observations are selected for success. There is no non-hit denominator, so frequency is not association and neither establishes causality. No regression, significance test, confidence interval or predictive score is defensible here. Repeated artists and recurring songs violate independent-row assumptions.

Era and market are stratified by source period; there is insufficient per-cell feature coverage for pooled inference. Duration has seven partially annotated entities; artist prior popularity, release exposure, spend and age are unobserved. Film status has affirmative cases and missing cases only. Multivariate adjustment is unidentifiable. A future sample should select releases before observing outcomes, include modest performers, and match market, calendar window, promotion scale, artist history and film context.

Official YouTube views, official upload age, views/day, watch hours, audience retention and repeat listening were not collected. Nothing is imputed from duration or annual equivalents. No claim is made that blocked/private fields were measured. One HSD song-report page timed out; search snippets from that page are not used as row measurements. No paid material was purchased or bypassed.

The analysis is a metadata-first feasibility study with meaningful but narrow chart evidence. It cannot fully answer which musical features are associated with exceptional performance across markets. That requires version-bound audio annotation plus a comparison cohort. All model outputs whose evidence is absent remain null.
