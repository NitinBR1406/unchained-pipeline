# SONG_SUCCESS_INTELLIGENCE_V01

**Decision:** use this study to choose and test production references, not to select a supposedly successful absolute key or predict a hit. The three blind tracks do not establish a reliable top-ten key/mode × BPM ranking. They support a bounded reference library, ten clearly labelled audition directions, a vocal-key testing protocol and a design for learning from UNCHAINED NITIN's own results.

All three reports were frozen before comparison. Gemini and Claude ran through authenticated provider surfaces; Track C ran as a separate GPT-based Codex research agent, not the consumer ChatGPT website. Their identical raw prompts, receipts, outputs and hashes are preserved separately. The coordinator verified the received bytes; B and C computation replays reproduced their outputs. **A successful hash or replay does not certify a claim.**

Provider reports use 22 September 2026 as their observation date. The coordinator's three-track receipt is 21 September 2026 23:39:49 UTC, already 22 September in Amsterdam. Provider calendar labels are retained as reported; they are not substituted for precise source-counter timestamps. See [delivery index](DELIVERY_INDEX_V01.json), [freeze gate](../THREE_TRACK_FREEZE_GATE.json) and [cross-validation matrix](CROSS_VALIDATION_MATRIX_V01.json).

## Global findings

**Diversity is observed; superiority is not.** In B's 25-row enriched cell, source-reported BPM has median 110 and a range of 75–190. The bands are five below 90, seven at 90–109, six at 110–129, five at 130–149 and two at 150 or above. These bins use the received numerical beat definitions, which are not standardized: they must not be treated as a perceptual-tempo distribution. A site's generic half/double-time suggestion is not evidence that listeners perceive both pulses equally.

The same selected cell reports 14 major, eight minor, two modal and one unknown. Across 25 rows that is 56%, 32%, 8% and 4%; among the 24 known labels the denominator is different. The most frequent reported tonic, D, occupies only five rows and mixes modes. None of this establishes an absolute-key advantage. B's 7/14 disagreement calculation compares **binary mode labels** between selected database and arrangement sources; it is not a complete tonic/key error rate. We retain competing labels instead of electing an original key by model majority.

B's reported duration median is 224 seconds. C's recent chart evidence and external structural reporting include other durations and pacing. The contrast is confounded by era, platform, selection and recording version. A shorter song cannot be declared more successful from these samples.

Annual IFPI outcomes provide a more coherent time window than all-time views, but use source-defined weighted and aggregated consumption. They are not literal Spotify play counts or release-age adjustment. [IFPI methodology and 2024 chart](https://www.ifpi.org/benson-boones-beautiful-things-confirmed-by-ifpi-as-biggest-selling-global-single-of-the-year/)

For structural context, HSD's nine non-holiday US number-one songs in 2025 include first choruses beyond one minute; its luther case places the first chorus at 1:09. This is evidence against a universal early-chorus deadline, **not** evidence that delaying a chorus improves retention. Its narrow US cohort must remain separate from global inference. [HSD report](https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Hit-Songs-Deconstructed-2025-1-Hit-Report.pdf)

The coordinator separately checked HSD's Blinding Lights analysis: it describes a returning instrumental motif and relationships with vocal motifs. This supports a structural study exercise—introduce and develop an original motif—but supplies no causal retention result. It is a post-freeze source check, not a fourth blind track. [HSD case study](https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/blinding-lights-the-art-of-the-hook.pdf)

## Hindi/Indian findings

The Indian cohorts are multilingual and must not be relabelled Hindi-only. In B's 40 video-source rows, exactly 24 have the label Hindi; 19 of those are labelled film-associated (79.2%). The frozen report's 20/27 calculation instead includes mixed Hindi/Punjabi and Hindi/Bengali rows. These are source-labelled upload frequencies, not estimates of film songs' probability of success. Film status for many streaming rows remains unknown.

B's Spotify India totals describe days present in the daily chart, not lifetime streams. Long chart occupancy and high peaks offer different descriptive signals; they do not reveal individual replay rates or the musical cause of longevity. The source lists are truncated, differently dated and use different definitions. Their limited overlap cannot establish that YouTube rewards dance while Spotify rewards ballads. [Spotify India chart-day totals](https://kworb.net/spotify/country/in_daily_totals.html)

C identifies source-linked musical estimates for only four useful Hindi/Indian references: Pehle Bhi Main, Maan Meri Jaan, Kesariya and Sajni. These include both major and minor labels and different tempos, with remaining beat-unit/version uncertainty. The metadata supports auditions, not a preferred mode, tonal center, or emotional formula. C's success-source selection also includes India consumption of non-Hindi repertoire. [2023 India reporting](https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/bollywood/spotify-wrapped-2023-arijit-singh-becomes-most-streamed-artist-9047077/), [Spotify 2024 winner](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2024-12-06/go-inside-the-global-celebrations-for-spotifys-biggest-wrapped-yet/)

The historical Indian scale study reviewed in B concerns 310 selected hits across 1953–2013, with composer-enriched sampling and limited double annotation. It reports scale-category frequencies and explicitly cautions against strong inference. Thaat/raga-related categories cannot simply be collapsed into a modern Western major/minor success comparison. No present-day Indian-versus-Western mode advantage is accepted. [Primary ISMIR paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2013/paper/000103.pdf)

## Crossover findings

B found no Indian intersection in its selected global leader lists. C found eight named Indian export/diaspora examples from Spotify reporting. This is a **difference in inclusion criteria**, not evidence that one track disproves the other. Top-list absence is not absence of international listening; export presence does not establish adoption beyond diaspora audiences.

The practical crossover question is therefore experimental: can Nitin retain natural Hindi phrasing and emotional identity while trying an accessible rhythmic or structural device? An English phrase, Western tempo or imported production texture is not established as a commercial advantage. Keep language version, listener market and campaign exposure attached to each observation. [Spotify export evidence](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-04-15/indian-artists-are-reaching-more-global-fans-than-ever-before-and-the-data-proves-it/), [diaspora context](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2023-08-07/broadcasting-desi-music-to-new-homes-around-the-world/)

## Associations, causality and uncertainty

This study's own song samples are selected for success, lack adequate matched feature coverage and contain no ordinary-release control cohort. A frequency such as “seven selected hits sit in this numerical tempo bin” cannot tell us whether that bin outperforms alternatives. No adjusted musical-performance coefficient, causal effect, significance claim, predictive model or population confidence interval is reported. Giving one would imply an unsupported sampling model.

B's literature inventory includes historical observational evidence about repetition and musical typicality. The claim audit supports a conditional typicality association in a historical US chart model (main regression n=25,077), but not a universal recipe of “one unusual element.” [Primary typicality paper](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/25509/Mauskapf_productfeatures.pdf)

The repetition paper studies different chart outcomes separately. More chorus repetition and excessive word repetition cannot be compressed into one universal optimum. Its chart evidence is associative; the audit checked indexed primary passages because the direct PDF fetch failed. These are research motivations for controlled tests, not scoring weights. [Primary repetition paper](https://msbfile03.usc.edu/digitalmeasures/jnunes/intellcont/Power%20of%20Repetition%20Final-1.pdf)

Key unresolved confounders are prior artist popularity, film exposure, marketing, distribution, language/market, era, version, duration and platform packaging. B stratifies some era/language/film rows descriptively; C separates annual market windows. Neither has enough data for a credible combined adjustment. Missingness is highly selective: 0/83 Indian records in B have musical features, whereas its older global video successes received enrichment.

### Age-normalization boundary

B computes approximate lifetime views/day for 17 dated global videos and 36 dated Indian videos. Its exact counters come from undated pages, so a precise count-as-of date is missing. These ratios reduce one arithmetic accumulation difference but do not remove exposure, promotion, cohort, seasonality or age effects. They are **not launch velocity**. No watch hours are inferred by multiplying views by duration.

Future collection should use counter snapshots with known timestamps, exact official upload IDs, matched first-7/28-day windows where available and recent delta views/day separately from lifetime averages. Old songs without historical snapshots cannot be retroactively given launch metrics. Annual chart ranks are period-specific success signals, not interchangeable with upload-age-normalized views.

## Ten reference zones for production

The [Vatsal brief](VATSAL_ORIGINAL_PRODUCTION_REFERENCE_BRIEF_V01.md) provides ten unordered practical audition directions with global/Hindi references, original **reported** keys/BPM, explicit support counts, emotional targets, production experiments and voice tests. The [machine-readable zones](TOP_REFERENCE_ZONES_V01.json) provide row IDs and uncertainty for ingestion.

These are not ten statistically highest-performing combinations. A zone's supporting n counts selected examples with reported musical metadata, including disclosed conflicts; it is not an experiment count or an independent-study count. Hindi listening suggestions without measured metadata are not counted as zone support. We could not honestly supply three to five verified Hindi musical matches per zone. All canonical recording keys and BPMs in the curated registry remain null pending version/audio validation; the reported values are preserved separately for audition planning.

Emotional and production profiles in the brief are **proposed original-song treatments**, not retroactively measured properties of every reference. Z01–Z03 are a manageable first-session shortlist chosen editorially, not ranked by a success model.

## Vocal key and transposition

Nitin's measured comfortable tessitura, sustained chorus demands and repeatability are unknown. The optimizer therefore returns no recommended absolute key or numerical fit. A reference's key describes that reference; it does not prescribe Nitin's key.

Uniform integer-semitone transposition preserves pitch intervals and mode, while the singer's register behavior, vowels and instrumental tone may change. Compare complete original chorus/climax phrases in a comfortable baseline and nearby keys, with comparable tempo/gain and rest. Judge sustained delivery and repeatability rather than the highest isolated note. Published male and female arrangement ranges must not be pooled into Nitin's target tessitura. See [optimizer specification](VOCAL_KEY_OPTIMIZER_V01.json).

## Factory feature and 10M attribution connection

[SONG_SUCCESS_INTELLIGENCE_ENGINE_V01.json](SONG_SUCCESS_INTELLIGENCE_ENGINE_V01.json) is an additive design specification, not a deployed engine. Inputs include the requested musical fields and detailed, version-bound structural annotations. Each value retains method, source, units, confidence, observation time and missing reason. Unknown means null, never zero.

- `SUCCESS_SIGNAL_SCORE`: null now. If later used, a defined retrospective outcome percentile within a declared platform/market/window—not a musical hit probability.
- `EVIDENCE_CONFIDENCE`: structured coverage, source quality, version binding, independence, agreement and bias dimensions.
- `REFERENCE_MATCH_SCORE`: similarity with explicit weights and missingness, never a forecast of success.
- `NITIN_FIT_SCORE`: null until auditions and a scoring protocol exist.
- `EXPECTED_RETENTION_PROFILE`: null until observed curves and a properly validated model support it. Five curves alone do not justify a forecast.
- `RISKS` and `COMPARABLE_SONGS`: traceable measurement limitations and references, with no automatic penalty for a long song, late chorus or key change.

The design binds musical parameter versions to `CONTENT_MASTER.song_id/content_id`; derivative hypotheses to `DERIVATIVES.experiment_id/hypothesis_id`; platform observations through `PUBLICATION → PLATFORM_PACKAGES → DERIVATIVES → CONTENT_MASTER → CAMPAIGNS`; and business outcomes through the existing `BUSINESS_FUNNEL`/`MONETIZATION` publication/funnel joins. Attribution evidence and unknown links remain explicit. Joining a song to a booking does not prove that its BPM caused the booking. The existing 10M architecture is reused as an attribution design, not claimed as a result.

No canonical production schema, asset, publication row or approval flag was changed. Production deployment and publication remain unauthorized; First Real Poster remains paused.
